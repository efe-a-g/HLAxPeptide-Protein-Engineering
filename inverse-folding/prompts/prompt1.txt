# Peptide-HLA stability prediction with protein foundation models

## Context

We are answering the Serova Protein Engineering Track question: can existing
protein foundation models improve prediction of peptide-HLA class I complex
stability over a hand-engineered supervised baseline?

Data: 28,166 rows, columns `allele`, `peptide` (all 9-mers), `thalf_hours`
(target), `hla_seq` (alpha1/alpha2 domain, ~182 aa), `hla_pseudoseq` (34
peptide-contacting polymorphic positions used by the NetMHCpan family).
~75 distinct alleles.

Biological intuition driving the design:
- Stability is dissociation half-life, not affinity. The peptide is a
  structural subunit of the complex, not a ligand sitting on it.
- The class I groove is closed at both ends by *invariant* residue clusters
  (Tyr7/59/159/171 clamping the peptide N-terminus; Thr143/Lys146/Trp147 the
  C-terminus). These bind the peptide backbone and carry much of the binding
  energy.
- The pseudosequence was built by keeping positions that are peptide-contacting
  AND polymorphic. It therefore *deliberately discards* the conserved clamp.
  Structural methods see information the baseline provably lacks. This is the
  central hypothesis.
- Side chains alternate: P2 and P9 point down into the polymorphic B and F
  pockets (the anchors, which dominate stability); P1/P3/P5/P7 point up and are
  solvent-exposed (these drive T-cell recognition, not stability).
- Peptide backbone conformation is near-identical across peptides AND across
  alleles (Ca RMSD < 1 Angstrom), because the clamps fix both ends. Alleles
  differ in pocket side-chain chemistry, not main-chain geometry. This is what
  makes template-based threading valid.

The goal is a defensible empirical answer with evidence, not a leaderboard
score. A well-supported negative result is a valid outcome and should be
reported as such.

## Phase 0 — Data audit (do this first, report before proceeding)

1. Distribution of `thalf_hours`; count exact zeros and near-zeros. The
   NetMHCstab lineage padded training data with weak binders (>20,000 nM)
   *assigned* half-life 0. Test whether a zero-mass subpopulation exists here:
   check clustering by allele, and whether P2/P9 composition of zero-rows looks
   like non-binders versus the measured rows.
2. Identify repeated (allele, peptide) pairs. Compute agreement between
   replicates. **This is the empirical noise ceiling on achievable correlation
   and must appear in the final report**, marked on every correlation plot.
3. Allele frequency distribution; flag imbalance.
4. Recover the 34 pseudosequence indices: find the index set S, |S|=34, with
   `hla_seq[S] == hla_pseudoseq` for every allele simultaneously. Over-determined
   across ~75 alleles, so S should be unique. Save it.
5. Per-allele empirical motifs: 9x20 position weight matrices computed from
   high-stability peptides only. Sanity check against known biology (A*02:01
   should prefer L/M at P2, V/L at P9; B*27:05 near-absolute R at P2; A*03:01
   K/R at P9). Save these — compared against structure-predicted motifs in
   Phase 3.

### Target handling

Model `log(thalf_hours + eps)`.

The zeros are **left-censored**, not measurements: the assay fits an exponential
to a decay curve, and if dissociation completes before the first timepoint the
honest statement is "t_half < c" for some assay resolution c. Under plain MSE a
censored row is fitted as though it equalled the floor, so the model spends
capacity on an artefact — and the floor's position depends on the arbitrary eps.

Required: report every headline metric twice, on all rows and on the measured
(non-zero) subset only. A model can post a good overall Spearman purely by
separating zeros from non-zeros, which is binding classification, not kinetics.

If the censored fraction is large, add a Tobit loss: squared error for
uncensored rows, and `max(0, y_hat - log(c))^2` for censored rows, so a
prediction below the threshold is free and only over-prediction is penalised.

## Phase 1 — Splits and metrics

NetMHCstabpan is trained on the entirety of this dataset and is NOT a valid
comparator. Do not use it as a baseline. Build our own.

Three splits, fixed seeds:
- `random` — included only to demonstrate leakage, never as a headline result.
- `peptide_disjoint` — no peptide in both train and test. Note the same peptide
  appears with multiple alleles at very different half-lives.
- `leave_one_allele_out` (LOAO) — hold out whole alleles. **This is the primary
  evaluation.** Allele representation only matters in the extrapolation regime;
  on a random split almost any encoding works.

If time permits, add similarity-reduced partitioning (Hobohm-1 on peptides).

**Primary metric: per-allele Spearman, then averaged across alleles.**
Spearman = Pearson on ranks; it measures monotone agreement, is invariant to
monotone reparametrisation of either variable, and is robust to the heavy right
tail and to the large tied block of censored zeros (use scipy's tie-averaging).
Rank is also what the application needs — the brief frames the problem as
ranking which peptides stably bind a given allele.

Pooled Spearman must NOT be the headline: it mixes between-allele and
within-allele variation, so it rewards merely knowing which allele you are
looking at. Computed within allele, the allele-mean baseline scores zero by
construction, which is the point. Report both size-weighted and unweighted
averages — they will differ given the allele imbalance.

Also report Pearson on log target, and AUC at a t_half > 2h threshold.

## Phase 2 — Baseline ladder

Fix the prediction head, the split, and the training budget. Vary only the
representation. Each rung isolates one claim.

1. **Per-allele mean of log target.** No peptide information. Measures how much
   total variance is between-allele rather than within. Tells you what a pooled
   correlation is actually worth. Compute means on train folds only.

2. **Per-allele position weight matrix.** Score a peptide as
   `sum_i log(f_a(x_i, i) / f_bg(x_i))`. The classical binding motif; assumes
   positional independence, which is close to true here since two anchors
   dominate with largely independent chemistry. **This is a genuinely strong
   baseline and is consistently underestimated** — if the foundation-model
   pipeline doesn't clearly beat it, we don't have a result. Fit on train folds
   only; use pseudocounts or BLOSUM-weighted smoothing (rare alleles will have
   positions where some amino acid never appears). Fails entirely under LOAO,
   which is informative, not a defect.

3. **Learned allele embedding.** `nn.Embedding(n_alleles, d)` + BLOSUM peptide,
   same head. The allele is a bare categorical with no sequence information.
   **Expected: matches or slightly beats the pseudosequence on peptide-disjoint
   splits (hundreds of examples per allele is ample to learn a task embedding),
   and cannot produce a prediction at all under LOAO.** This control isolates
   what the pan representation actually buys — not accuracy on seen alleles, but
   existence of a prediction on unseen ones. It reframes the project: results on
   random or peptide-disjoint splits are near-uninformative about allele
   representation, and LOAO is where the real question lives.

4. **BLOSUM peptide (9x20) + one-hot pseudosequence (34x20) -> small MLP.**
   The NetMHCstabpan-shaped baseline and the thing to beat.

Also try a pairwise ranking loss within allele as an alternative to absolute
regression. It sidesteps allele offsets, assay batch structure, and the censored
bottom of the range (all censored rows are simply tied at the bottom), and it
optimises the metric we report. This may matter more than any representation
choice.

## Phase 3 — Inverse folding (primary differentiated experiment)

Rationale: inverse-folding models give p(sequence | backbone). Conditioning on
the groove geometry and scoring the observed peptide yields a zero-shot
stability proxy that uses the conserved clamp and pocket geometry the
pseudosequence discards. No training, no labels, so immune to every split
concern, and naturally pan-specific.

Use **LigandMPNN** (better atomic context handling, on Serova's model list);
fall back to ProteinMPNN if setup is slow.

### Template construction
- Pull 5-10 high-resolution class I structures with **9-mer** peptides from the
  PDB, spanning different alleles. (PDB 4MJI, cited in the brief, is an 8-mer on
  B*51:01 — do not use it as the sole template.)
- Strip to backbone (N, CA, C, O). HLA and peptide as separate chains in one
  file — the peptide must be a spatially adjacent chain, not appended to the HLA
  sequence.
- Decide empirically whether to truncate the HLA to alpha1/alpha2 (matches our
  data exactly, but leaves an unnatural exposed interface where alpha3 was) or
  keep the full assembly with alpha3 and beta2-microglobulin. **Test both on a
  500-row subset before committing** — this can quietly degrade everything
  downstream.

### Two scoring modes, both required — they are different experiments

**(b) UNCONDITIONAL — run this first.** Peptide sequence is NOT an input. One
forward pass per allele (~75 total) over backbone + fixed `hla_seq`, giving a
9x20 matrix per allele: the model's geometry-derived prediction of what each
groove wants at each position. Score any peptide by lookup and sum. Assumes
positional independence. ~400x cheaper than (a), essentially free.

Immediately plot these predicted motifs against the empirical motifs from
Phase 0.5. **Does structure alone recover B*27:05's P2 arginine preference?**
This is the single most convincing figure if it works, and tells you within an
hour whether the structural channel carries real signal. If these motifs are
garbage, (a) will not rescue the approach.

**(a) CONDITIONAL (autoregressive).** Peptide sequence IS an input. Per row:
`sum_i log p(x_i | x_<i, backbone, hla_seq)`. Captures inter-position
dependence — how the choice at P2 shifts what is tolerated at P9. 28k forward
passes, minutes of compute.

Setup: `--chain_id_jsonl` marks the HLA chain fixed and the peptide designable;
`--score_only` evaluates the supplied sequence rather than sampling a new one.
Decoding order is randomised, so scores wobble between runs — average over
several random orders and over the template ensemble, and report the spread, not
just a point estimate. Verify current flag names against the repo rather than
trusting these.

**Report the gap between (a) and (b).** If (a) barely beats (b), class I binding
is close to positionally independent — the implicit assumption behind every PWM
in the field, here tested directly against a model capable of violating it.
That is a result worth stating either way.

### Features extracted
From the same forward passes:
- `mpnn_mean_logp` — scalar.
- `mpnn_logp_per_position` — 9 values. Expected to beat the scalar, since a sum
  dilutes the P2/P9 signal across seven positions that barely matter.
- `mpnn_anchor_softmax` — full 20-way distributions at P2 and P9 (40 values).
  Likely the most informative: the model's statement of what the pocket wants,
  which is what the pseudosequence is a proxy for, arrived at independently.

Include all three and ablate; the marginal cost is zero.

### Mandatory controls
- **Scrambled-HLA control.** Re-score with `hla_seq` shuffled. If scores barely
  move, the model is reading peptide sequence alone and the structural framing
  is decoration. **Run this before building anything on top.**
- **Per-position log-prob profile.** Plot mean log-prob across P1-P9. Should
  show the model most constrained at P2 and P9. A flat profile means anchor
  chemistry isn't being captured.
- Where real PDB structures exist for our alleles, compare shared-template
  scoring against per-allele structures on those alleles. Justifies the shortcut
  with evidence rather than assertion.

### Reporting
Three rows in the results table:
1. Structural score alone, zero-shot, no training.
2. Phase 2 baseline alone.
3. Baseline + structural features.

Row 3 is only interesting relative to row 2 — it tests whether the structural
information is orthogonal to the hand-crafted encoding. If it adds nothing, the
information was already there, which is a clean result with a mechanism.

## Engineering

- Seed everything; log all runs with config.
- Cache MPNN scores to disk keyed by (template, allele, peptide, seed) — do not
  recompute.
- Build the full pipeline on a 1,000-row stratified subset first (uniform
  sampling gives mostly A*02:01), verify end to end, then scale.
- Standardise structural features before concatenation — they will otherwise be
  swamped by an ~860-dim one-hot baseline vector. Consider a separate branch
  merged late in the head.
